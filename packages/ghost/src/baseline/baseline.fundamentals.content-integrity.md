## Usage

Defaults for matching certainty to evidence. Brand character changes how a fact is expressed, not what the evidence supports.

## Rules

- Present claims, metrics, prices, specifications, availability, scarcity, source history, certifications, and product behavior as facts only when supported.
- Omit or qualify a claim when evidence is missing; label illustrative claims clearly.
- Mark proposals, provisional statements, examples, schematic or placeholder data, and unresolved facts as such.
- When credible sources conflict, the work states the conflict or omits the disputed claim rather than selecting the convenient answer.
- **Default; required where legally mandated.** State fees, limits, risks, consent, and irreversible consequences plainly before commitment.
- No synthetic, altered, or placeholder media is presented as documentary proof of a real person, place, product, or event.
- Where authentic material is missing, the work names the gap rather than using a fabricated substitute.
- Every color, icon, component, logo, chart series, and type value presented as brand guidance traces to supplied brand materials or a documented source.
- Where no record exists for such a value, the work names the gap rather than supplying a plausible one.
- A named gap is a report to whoever directed the work rather than rendered content: put it in a comment in the output's own source syntax, and never in rendered copy such as a caption, footer, or eyebrow.
- Unrequested reasoning behind a brand choice goes in a source comment or nowhere.
- Where explaining brand rules is the requested content, such as a brand guide or a design-system explainer, that explanation is the work itself.
- What a reader needs to judge the content, such as a label marking schematic data, stays visible.
- Promotional language rests on a concrete, relevant fact.
- Real scarcity is stated plainly, with the actual limit or date where one exists.
- Routine confirmations stay factual and proportional; reserve exclamation-heavy success copy, confetti language, and “You’re all set!” for a real milestone or announcement that warrants celebration.

## Never

- Never write error copy that blames the person — instead identify the problem and give a usable recovery action.
- Never manufacture urgency or scarcity to move someone — instead state the real availability plainly.
- Never substitute interchangeable superlatives for a concrete fact that is available — instead state the fact.
