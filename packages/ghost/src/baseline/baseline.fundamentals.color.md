## Usage

Color is relational: an isolated swatch cannot predict its appearance beside other colors, imagery, or text. Palette decisions start from purpose, mood, content, medium, and the brand's supplied choices. Established combinations can inform exploration without becoming fixed production formulas.

Proportion and intensity establish focus. Value provides the foundation of readability, while hue, saturation, and temperature can reinforce hierarchy and mood. A scarce accent has more signaling power than an accent distributed everywhere.

## Rules

- Assign each color a functional, conceptual, emotional, or brand role; keep the same semantic meaning across related work. Distinguish backgrounds, surfaces, text, accents, boundaries, and status roles where present.
- Use the fewest colors that communicate the idea. For display work, begin with one dominant family, one or two supporting colors, and an accent when useful.
- Give dominant, supporting, and accent areas visibly different weight. Judge proportions optically and functionally; 60/30/10 can start exploration but is never a universal validation rule.
- Evaluate the complete palette at intended scale against real backgrounds, adjacent colors, text weights, and image crops.
- Balance saturated colors with quieter colors or neutrals, varying their occupied area; consider reducing saturation before adding another color.
- Give neutrals a deliberate warm, cool, or balanced temperature. Use temperature to support depth, focus, or mood, with strong contrast concentrated where it matters.
- Use gradients for a defined concept, hierarchy, depth, transition, or separation; test the actual regions behind the overlaid content.

## Never

- Never use all available palette colors merely because they exist — instead select by role.
- Never give every high-chroma color equal emphasis without a conceptual purpose — instead establish distinct proportions.
- Never highlight everything or repeat the accent until it stops signaling — instead reserve emphasis for priority.
- Never approve a palette from isolated swatches or color-wheel geometry alone — instead assess the composition in context.
- Never mix nearly identical warm and cool neutrals accidentally — instead choose their relationship deliberately.
- Never add gradients to imply modernity or color to disguise a weak layout — instead resolve hierarchy and use color for a defined purpose.
