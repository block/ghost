## Usage

Defaults for reading duration, line breaking, and type detail. A dense interface, long article, and large-format headline need different settings.

## Rules

- Standard body lines run 45 to 75 characters; sustained reading targets 60 to 66.
- Narrow columns on small screens run 35 to 55 characters; short display copy runs 20 to 55. When categories overlap, use the range for the more specific setting: a narrow small-screen column uses 35 to 55 even when it contains sustained reading.
- When line length falls outside its range, change the container width, type size, copy length, or column count. Avoid columns that repeatedly leave only one or two words on a line.
- Code, data tables, mathematical expressions, captions, navigation, and deliberately expressive display type are exempt from these character-count ranges; set their line length for their content and purpose.
- These character-count ranges apply to languages written with the Latin alphabet, including English, Spanish, French, and German. For other writing systems, set line length according to their reading and line-breaking conventions.
- Screen body line spacing starts at 1.3 to 1.6 times the type size, print body line spacing at 1.2 to 1.45, and display line spacing at 0.9 to 1.15.
- Adjust line spacing from that starting point for the specific face, its x-height, the weight, the line length, and the language rather than applying one ratio to every size and role.
- Increase line spacing as lines grow wider, and tighten it as display type grows larger where the letterforms allow.
- One repeated content role maintains one line height.
- Where a vertical increment exists, derive it from body line spacing so repeated text aligns to the same vertical intervals.
- Three primary type sizes and three weights is the working ceiling for a single view; use two when two distinguish the content roles.
- Give every size and weight a stated content role.
- Each type level is visibly distinct at the intended output size and viewing distance: use a jump of a step or two, not a drop to the bottom of the scale.
- Keep one size per recurring role throughout an artifact; an opening cover title or page hero may use a larger step than the titles that follow.
- Dense interfaces, editorial systems, and data displays can use additional sizes when each has a named, recurring role.
- Size distinguishes information levels; weight, underline, color, spacing, and position emphasize content within a level.
- Use one emphasis device at a time.
- In interfaces, underline is reserved for links, interactive text, and explicitly defined states.
- In non-interactive brand, editorial, and display work, underline is available as deliberate emphasis where weight alone does not distinguish it.
- Underlines use a consistent weight and offset and clear descenders without collision.
- Centered display copy stays within three lines.
- Full justification is available only where line length, word spacing, and hyphenation are actively controlled, with no visible rivers, holes, or extreme word spacing.
- Use paragraph spacing or first-line indentation, not both.
- The space below a heading stays smaller than the space above it so the heading groups with the content it introduces.
- Separate a title and subtitle with a spacing step in addition to their line-height.
- Ensure headlines and display lines break on meaning and natural phrasing rather than accepting what the container edge produces.
- A name, a number and its unit, a date, or a product name does not break across lines without necessity.
- The rag stays natural, without a repeated short-long-short pattern.
- Hyphenate selectively where it materially improves the rag or a justified setting, never across more than two consecutive lines, and not in names, URLs, headlines, or display copy.
- A paragraph set larger or otherwise emphasized does not end with a one-word final line.
- At least two lines of a paragraph stay together at a column or page break.
- A heading stays with at least two lines of the content it introduces.
- Recompose a headline for each language rather than forcing the original line endings onto translated copy.
- Start from the face's native spacing and keep body tracking at or near its default.
- Reserve all caps for short labels and deliberate display type, with modest positive tracking where it aids legibility.
- Retest a numeric type size when the same setting moves between print, mobile, desktop, and large-format work, and increase size as viewing distance grows.
- Use a stronger weight when reversed, small, or low-resolution settings thin the letterforms.
- Adjacent lines remain visually distinct.
- Ascenders, descenders, accents, and punctuation do not collide.
- Bold, italic, condensed, and expanded text uses a style supported by the typeface rather than one simulated by the rendering software.
- When embedding fonts, declare a font-face rule covering every weight used so the browser loads the drawn face rather than synthesizing it.
- Every fallback stack includes the weights the hierarchy depends on.
- The rendered face includes every character, punctuation mark, symbol, currency, diacritic, and script the content requires.
- Essential text remains readable when reversed, small, or rendered at low resolution.
- Fine punctuation, accents, counters, and thin strokes survive at final size.

## Never

- Never stretch, skew, or otherwise distort letterforms — instead use a typeface style that has the needed width or angle, or change the size, line length, or copy.
- Never introduce a type size to emphasize a single word, phrase, or label — instead use an existing weight, underline, color, spacing, or position.
- Never reduce type size below its reading job to force copy into a fixed container — instead edit the copy or change the container.
- Never widen a text block only to occupy unused space — instead preserve a readable line length and let the space stand.
- Never insert manual line breaks inside body paragraphs — instead let them reflow, and insert breaks only in headlines and display copy.
- Never underline non-interactive interface text that could then read as clickable — instead signify the emphasis with weight, size, or color.
- Never set long paragraphs in all caps — instead set them in sentence case and reserve all caps for short labels.
- Never use shadows, outlines, glows, bevels, gradients, or dimensional treatments as a default text treatment or in place of hierarchy — instead establish hierarchy through scale, weight, and space.
- Never tightly track small body copy or apply heavy positive tracking to lowercase text — instead keep the face's native spacing.
- Never apply emphasis often enough that it becomes the base style — instead reduce it until the emphasized text is the exception.
