## Usage

A crop is part of the message: representative subject matter and aspect ratios reveal whether an arrangement will work before final assets arrive. The image's own quiet areas can support type or marks without competing with its subject.

## Rules

- Crop to strengthen subject, hierarchy, and composition; protect important faces, products, gestures, text, and narrative focal points.
- Preserve image aspect ratio unless intentional distortion serves the concept; use focal-point metadata or art-directed crops for responsive variants.
- Align image edges to the established grid or make the break deliberate, and size images in whole grid-field combinations where useful.
- Use scale, position, sequence, or contrast to establish relationships in multi-image work; equal sizes need an intentional repetitive structure.
- Place overlaid text in quiet, tonally stable image areas. When the crop cannot support it, create separation through a solid field, scrim, controlled gradient, blur, or alternate crop.

## Never

- Never center-crop every image automatically — instead protect its focal subject in each format.
- Never hide poor source quality with sharpening, noise, blur, or artificial texture — instead obtain a suitable source or change the composition.
- Never let an overlay hide a face, product, gesture, or narrative focal point — instead move the overlay or change the crop.
