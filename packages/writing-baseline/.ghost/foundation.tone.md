Baseline chapter: the moments are usage law; the range is a known gap.

## Usage

Voice stays constant; tone moves with the reader's moment. The same brand is
briefer in a routine task, steadier in a failure, and slower in a crisis.
Before writing, read the moment: what just happened to the person, what is at
stake for them, and how much attention they have to spare.

When a moment is unclear, choose the quieter tone. Too little warmth in a
routine moment costs nothing; misplaced cheer in a bad moment costs trust.

## Rules

- Routine task: instructive. Neutral, brief, and scannable; the action and
  nothing else.
- Decision point, such as pricing, consent, or a setting with consequences:
  guiding. State the options, what each costs or changes, and which can be
  undone.
- Error or blocked task: guiding. The fact and the next step, without cheer.
- Critical moment, such as lost money, a locked account, a security event,
  lost data, or an outage: supportive. Acknowledge the stakes, explain only
  what the person needs to act, and say when they will hear more. No humor.
- Milestone the person would mark themselves: celebration is allowed, once and
  briefly. A routine success is a confirmation, not a milestone.
- Known gap: range. How far each tone moves for this brand, such as how warm
  the supportive tone runs and whether celebration appears at all, is the
  brand's answer. Ask the human; until answered, stay at the quiet end of each
  tone and label it provisional.

## Never

- Never celebrate the routine ("Awesome, you saved it!"); instead confirm the
  fact ("Changes saved").
- Never joke in a critical moment; instead state what happened and what
  happens next.
- Never offer reassurance the facts do not support ("Don't worry, everything's
  fine"); instead say what is known and what is being done.
- Never hold one tone across every moment; instead read the moment first.
