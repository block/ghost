---
for: Writing or editing headlines, marketing, product copy, or any first draft where generic phrasing could stand in for a decision.
---

Shared copy defaults: the phrasing models and hurried writers reach for when
nobody has made a decision. Every rule below is a **Default**, a starting
position that a brand's explicit guidance may deliberately replace. Each rule
is reject → replace. When a brand replaces one, delete the rule and its paired
check flag together.

Each rule names its provenance. **Ghost default** marks a pattern Ghost
already ships in `standard.model-defaults` or its median-tells check, with the
measured count from unsteered generations where one is published.
**Editorial** marks widely shared editorial practice that has not been
measured. If this package also carries `standard.model-defaults`, its Stock
copy, Celebration, and Eyebrow kicker rules are the same decisions; keep one
copy of each.

### Stock headings
Reject headings that would work unchanged for a competitor ("Simple,
transparent pricing", "Welcome back", "Everything you need to grow") → a
heading that names what this product does, with a specific noun. Test: swap
in a competitor's name; if the line still works, rewrite it. (Ghost default;
measured: 14 for "Simple, transparent pricing", 10 for "Welcome back".)

### Celebration
Reject exclamation-marked success and confetti language on routine outcomes
→ a quiet, factual confirmation. (Ghost default.)

### Aphoristic cadence
Reject the repeated "Not X. Y." or "Sentence. No qualifiers." rhythm → plain
sentences, with a fragment used once where it earns emphasis. (Ghost
default.)

### Em-dash pileups
Reject five or more em dashes in one view → commas, periods, or parentheses,
keeping a dash only for a real interruption. (Ghost default.)

### Eyebrow kicker
Reject a small uppercase label above every section heading → at most one,
where it names something. (Ghost default.)

### Hype vocabulary
Reject "unlock", "seamless", "elevate", "supercharge", "effortless",
"empower", "revolutionize", "game-changing", and "next-level" → the concrete
thing that changes for the reader. (Editorial.)

### Empty superlatives
Reject "best-in-class", "world-class", "cutting-edge", and
"industry-leading" without evidence → the specific fact the superlative
stands in for, or nothing. (Editorial.)

### Corporate register
Reject "utilize", "leverage", "facilitate", "please be advised", "at this
time", and "in order to" → "use", "help", "now", and "to". (Editorial.)

### Vague nouns
Reject "solution", "experience", "robust", "feature-rich", "stuff", and
"things" where they do not name what the product does → the noun that does.
(Editorial.)

### Absolute promises
Reject "guaranteed", "always", "never", "perfect", "100% secure", and "no
risk" unless the claim is literally true and approved → the precise, bounded
claim. (Editorial.)

### Hedge stacks
Reject stacked hedges ("may potentially help", "you might want to consider")
→ a decision: state the instruction or the limit plainly. (Editorial.)

### Interface apology
Reject "Oops!", "Uh oh", and "Sorry, something went wrong" → the fact and the
next step. Apologize only when the organization caused real harm, once and
specifically. (Editorial.)

### Rhetorical openers
Reject opening with a question ("Ready to take your business to the next
level?", "Tired of juggling tools?") or with "Whether you're X or Y" → open
with the claim. (Editorial.)

### Reflexive triads
Reject lists of three chosen for rhythm ("fast, simple, and secure") when
the evidence does not support all three → the one or two the evidence
supports. (Editorial.)

### Generic actions
Reject "Get started", "Submit", and "Continue" where a specific verb exists →
the action itself ("Create invoice", "Send request"). (Editorial.)

### People as users
Reject "users" in copy addressed to or about people → "you", or the role the
person plays ("customers", "members", "admins"). (Editorial.)
