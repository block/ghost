Baseline chapter: consistency is usage law; the style choices are known gaps.

## Usage

A reader notices inconsistency before they notice style. Mechanics are
decisions made once and applied everywhere, so the brand's choice matters
less than holding it. The conventions below are widely shared; a brand may
replace any of them with an explicit answer.

Write for scanning and truncation. Readers skim, and interfaces cut text
short, so the word that carries the meaning goes first.

## Rules

- One style per surface: every string on a surface follows the same case,
  punctuation, and format choices.
- Point of view stays fixed within an experience. Do not switch between "your
  account" and "my account" in one flow.
- Lead with the meaningful word: "Payment failed: card expired", not "We're
  sorry to let you know that your payment could not be processed."
- Emphasis marks only the thing to act on or the fact that changes a decision.
- Convention: buttons, labels, headings, links, menu items, and placeholder
  text take no end period; complete sentences in body copy do.
- Convention: numerals for quantities in interface copy ("3 items").
- Money, dates, and times use one locale-correct format per surface. Avoid
  numeric dates such as 03/04 when readers may span locales.
- Known gap: case. Sentence case or title case for headings and buttons? Until
  answered, use sentence case and label it provisional.
- Known gap: punctuation. Serial comma or not; which dash and whether it is
  spaced; whether exclamation marks ever appear; whether "&" may replace
  "and". Until answered, use the serial comma, avoid exclamation marks, and
  label the choices provisional.
- Known gap: contractions. On by default, off, or only some? Until answered,
  use common contractions ("you're", "can't") and avoid unusual ones.
- Known gap: emoji. Never, or where and in what position? Until answered, use
  none.
- Known gap: formats. Currency placement, date and time order, and approved
  abbreviations. Until answered, follow the reader's locale.

## Never

- Never mix styles within a surface; instead pick one and apply it to every
  string.
- Never use capitals or bold across a whole sentence for emphasis; instead
  emphasize the one thing to act on.
- Never bury the meaning after a preamble; instead lead with it.
